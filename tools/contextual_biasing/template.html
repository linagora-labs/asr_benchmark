Some models accept a list of words to expect (names, drugs, companies...): what do they gain from it, and what does it cost?

Scores

  • Case sensitive: for WER, B-WER and U-WER, the case of all the words counts, compared with the reference (without case when the references have no case, as for LibriSpeech). For F1, precision and recall, a term is correct only if written as in the list given to the model (as in the targets for runs without list).
  • Punctuation: never counted. The marks are removed after the conversion of the numbers, acronyms...
  • Ranking: by WER; the models whose WER is within the 95% confidence interval of the best one form a level (between two lines), ranked by B-WER, and so on from the best of the others.

Models and columns

  • Hotword list (or a click on a list in the header of the leaderboard): the list the models are ranked on, in the leaderboard and the plots. The line under the name of a model gives its decoding (if several are compared) and the software that runs it.
  • Technique, how the model takes the list into account: Context, an input made for context, that the model is trained to use as background knowledge (Qwen3-ASR, VibeVoice-ASR); Prompt, the text prompt of the model (Granite, Nemotron, MOSS: with the template of their documentation; Whisper: the decoder prompt made for the previous text, at most 223 tokens); Boosting, scores of the terms raised at decoding time, without training (NeMo). Hover it for the text given.
  • Size (parameters), License and Architecture: as in the other tabs.
  • RTFx: on one NVIDIA DGX Spark (GB10), as the benchmark runs each model: vLLM servers with 32 to 128 concurrent requests, NeMo and VibeVoice-ASR in batches (64; 4 to 16 with the long lists), faster-whisper one file at a time. It compares the cost of the lists for a model more than the models with each other, and not with the RTFx of the other tabs. A list given in the prompt is processed with every request: the longer the list, the slower.

Data

  • Terms: all the terms to recognize are in the lists, with others that are not spoken. What happens when a spoken term is missing from the list is not measured.
  • Test sets “English” and “French and English”: mean over their test sets (each weighs the same), by size class of the lists: short (25 names, call keywords, 100 distractors), medium (389 names, 500 distractors), long (drug dictionaries, 1,000 and 2,000 distractors). In English, only LibriSpeech has medium and long lists: their values are not comparable with the ones of the short lists (compare the changes). In the line charts, a dotted line joins them, only to follow a model.
  • Two of the lists (Vidal, Thériaque) are private: the runs with them are published without their transcriptions.
  • Results and configuration: benchmarks/contextual_biasing.
